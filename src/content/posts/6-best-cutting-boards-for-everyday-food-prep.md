## How to choose a cutting board

### Wood, plastic, or composite?

Choose based on maintenance and how you cook:

* **Plastic or other nonporous synthetic boards** are convenient for raw proteins because many are dishwasher safe. They are light and affordable but should be replaced when deep grooves become difficult to clean.
* **Wood boards** provide a substantial cutting surface and can last for years with appropriate care. They need hand washing, prompt drying, and periodic oiling.
* **Paper-composite boards** are thin and low maintenance. Some, including the Epicurean model above, are dishwasher safe, but they feel and sound different under a knife than traditional wood.
* **Flexible mats** store easily and simplify transferring chopped ingredients. They work best for light prep or as secondary surfaces rather than as a single heavy-duty board.

Glass, marble, ceramic, and stone may be easy to wipe clean, but their hard surfaces are generally a poor match for maintaining a knife edge. They are better treated as serving or pastry surfaces than as everyday chopping boards.

### What size cutting board do you need?

A board should be large enough to hold the ingredient and the knife work without pushing food over the edges. Before buying, measure three places:

1. The counter area where you prepare food.
2. The sink, because the board still needs to be washed comfortably.
3. The cabinet, drawer, rack, or vertical space where the board will dry and live.

Small boards are convenient for fruit and quick garnishes. A medium board around 12 by 18 inches is more comfortable for most meal prep. Larger boards give you room to organize ingredients, but their weight and cleaning requirements can become inconvenient.

### Do you need a juice groove?

A perimeter groove is helpful when carving meat or cutting juicy fruit. It can keep liquid from reaching the counter, but it also reduces the completely flat working area and adds a channel that must be cleaned. A reversible board with a groove on one side and a flat surface on the other offers the most flexibility.

### How important are non-slip feet?

A moving cutting board is frustrating and potentially unsafe. Integrated rubber feet are convenient, but they can make a board usable on only one side. A reversible board can be stabilized by placing a clean, damp kitchen towel or a thin non-slip mat underneath it.

### Should you use separate cutting boards?

The USDA recommends using one board for fresh produce and a separate board for raw meat, poultry, and seafood when possible. Boards should be washed with hot, soapy water after preparing each food item, and excessively worn boards with hard-to-clean grooves should be replaced. The FDA similarly advises keeping raw foods separate and using a dedicated board or plate for them.

That does not necessarily mean buying a large color-coded set. Two clearly identifiable boards, one for raw proteins and one for ready-to-eat ingredients, can be enough for many households.

Sources: [USDA guidance on preventing cross-contamination](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/How-can-I-prevent-cross-contamination-when-using-cutting-boards) and [FDA food-safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/womens-health-topics/food-safety-home)

## How to care for a cutting board

* Follow the manufacturer’s cleaning instructions for the specific board.
* Wash the board after each food item with hot, soapy water.
* Use the dishwasher only when the manufacturer marks the board dishwasher safe.
* Let boards dry completely before stacking or storing them.
* Never soak a wood board or leave it wet in the sink.
* Apply food-grade mineral oil to an oil-finished wood board on the schedule recommended by its manufacturer.
* Replace boards with deep cracks, excessive wear, or grooves that cannot be cleaned thoroughly.
