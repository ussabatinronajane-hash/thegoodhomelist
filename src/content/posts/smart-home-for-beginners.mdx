Most smart home guides start by having you buy a hub, pick an ecosystem, and commit to a platform
before you have automated a single thing.

That is backwards. You do not yet know what you want automated, because almost nobody does until
they live with it. People who plan an entire smart home upfront end up with four apps, a drawer of
abandoned sensors, and a light switch their partner refuses to use.

Start with one device on something you already touch every day. Learn what annoys you. Then buy
the second thing.

## What a smart home is actually for

Three things, and only three, are worth automating at the start.

**Things you forget.** The iron, the curling wand, the space heater. A schedule that cuts power at
a fixed time removes a recurring low-grade worry.

**Things you do in the dark.** Hallways, stairs, the walk from the car. Motion-triggered light is
the single most used automation in most homes because it solves a problem you have daily.

**Things you do at fixed times.** Coffee at 6:40, porch light at dusk, bedroom lamp at bedtime.

Notice what is not on the list: voice control of everything, colour-changing mood scenes, and
telling your fridge what to order. Those are demos. The list above is what people still use two
years in.

## Start with one smart plug

A smart plug is the right first purchase for one reason. It costs under twenty dollars and it
tells you honestly whether you will use this stuff.

Put it on something with a hard on/off state: a lamp, a fan, a coffee maker, a space heater.
Schedule it. Live with it for two weeks.

If you find yourself wanting more, you have learned what to buy next and it will be the right
thing. If the novelty fades, you spent twenty dollars finding out instead of four hundred.

**What to look for in a first plug:**

- **15A rating**, which is the standard and matters if you ever switch anything that heats
- **Matter support**, so it works across Apple, Amazon and Google and survives you switching sides
- **Compact body**, or it blocks the second socket on the outlet
- **A reputation for reconnecting**, which is the thing that separates good from bad

That last point is the whole ballgame. Every plug works on day one. What matters is what happens
after your router reboots at 3am. Read the two and three star reviews and search for the word
"reconnect".

## What not to buy first

**A hub.** You do not need one until you have around ten devices or want things to keep working
during an internet outage. Buying one first is solving a problem you do not have.

**A smart lock.** High stakes, moderate benefit, and the failure mode is standing outside your own
house. Get there eventually, not first.

**Smart bulbs for a room you switch at the wall.** If someone flips the wall switch, the bulb
loses power and nothing works. This is the most common smart home disappointment. Either use a
smart switch instead, or accept the wall switch has to stay on permanently.

**A video doorbell as device number one.** It is genuinely useful, but it involves subscriptions,
mounting, and wiring. Not a first project.

## Motion sensor lights, the automation people actually keep

If the smart plug works out, this is the natural second step and the one people rate highest.

A motion sensor light in a hallway, a stairwell, under a kitchen cabinet or inside a closet solves
a real daily annoyance. You never think about it again, which is the mark of a good automation.

**What to look for:**

- **Adjustable sensitivity and timeout**, or it either misses you or stays on for ten minutes
- **Warm colour temperature**, since a cold white light at 2am is unpleasant
- **Battery or plug-in**, matched to where it goes. Battery for closets, plug-in for hallways
- **No hub requirement** for a standalone unit

## Choosing an ecosystem without regretting it

You will be nudged toward Alexa, Google Home or Apple Home. The honest answer is that it matters
less than it used to, because Matter devices work with all three.

Pick based on the phones and speakers you already own. If your household is on iPhones, Apple
Home. If you already have Echo speakers, Alexa. If you use Google on everything, Google Home.

Then buy Matter devices where you can. That is your escape hatch if you change your mind.

## A sane first-year path

| Stage | Buy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | One smart plug | Find out if you use it, cheaply |
| Month 2 | A second plug, or a motion light | Solve the annoyance you noticed |
| Month 3 to 6 | Smart bulbs or switches for one room | Do one room properly rather than every room badly |
| Month 6+ | A hub, if you have hit ten devices | Local control and offline reliability |

Nobody needs to follow this exactly. The point is the order: one device, then the problem you
noticed, then a room, then infrastructure. Not the reverse.

## The bottom line

Buy one Matter-compatible smart plug. Put it on something you already switch daily. Wait two
weeks.

Everything else in smart home follows from what you learn in those two weeks, and it will be
different from what you would have guessed.
