A smart plug is the cheapest upgrade in the entire smart home category. Twelve dollars turns a
lamp, a coffee maker or a fan into something you can schedule, automate and switch off from bed.

The problem is that most of them are the same white box from the same handful of factories, with
different logos and wildly different firmware quality. The difference between a good smart plug
and a bad one is not the hardware. It is whether it reconnects on its own after your router
reboots at 3am, or whether you find out three days later that your schedule silently stopped.

Here is what actually holds up.

## What to look for

**Amp rating first.** This is the one spec with a safety consequence. Standard plugs are rated
15A, which is about 1800W at 120V. A space heater at 1500W leaves almost no headroom. If you are
switching anything that produces heat, check the rating on the plug body and not the listing page.

**Reconnection behavior.** Every plug works on day one. What separates them is what happens after
a power cut or a firmware update. Read reviews filtered to the 2 and 3 star range and search for
the word "reconnect". That tells you more than any spec sheet.

**Physical size.** Many plugs are wide enough to cover both outlets on a duplex receptacle. If you
need two, buy a model explicitly described as mini, or plan on a short extension.

**Matter support.** Matter is the industry standard that lets a device work across Apple, Amazon
and Google without a bridge. Buying Matter now means the plug survives you switching ecosystems
later. It costs a few dollars more and is worth it.

## Who should skip smart plugs

If you want to control an overhead light, a smart bulb or a smart switch is the better tool. A
smart plug on a lamp still requires the lamp switch to stay on, which family members will turn
off, and then nothing works. Smart plugs are for appliances with a hard power state: fans,
heaters, coffee makers, Christmas lights, dehumidifiers, aquarium pumps.

They are also a poor fit for anything with a digital control panel that needs to be re-armed after
a power cut. Cutting power to a modern washing machine does not turn it off, it resets it.
