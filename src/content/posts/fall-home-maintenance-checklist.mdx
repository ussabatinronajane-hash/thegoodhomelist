Most fall maintenance checklists list thirty tasks, which is why most of them get ignored. Nobody
is dedicating a weekend to thirty things.

The useful version is shorter, because the tasks are not equally important. Almost every expensive
winter home repair traces back to one of two causes: water that got somewhere it should not, or
cold air that got in and warm air that got out.

So do the water and air jobs first. If you only get through five things, make it these five.

## The five that actually prevent damage

### 1. Clear the gutters, after the leaves drop

Blocked gutters send water down the exterior wall and into the foundation. In freezing climates
they also cause ice dams, which push water back up under the shingles and into the ceiling.

The timing trips people up. Clearing gutters in early October, before the trees are bare, means
doing it twice. Wait until the leaves are mostly down.

Check that downspouts discharge at least three feet from the foundation. A downspout emptying
against the wall undoes the whole job.

### 2. Shut off and drain outdoor faucets

The single cheapest job with the most expensive failure mode. Water left in an exterior pipe
expands when it freezes and splits the pipe, which you discover in spring when you turn it on and
water pours into the wall.

Shut the interior valve, then open the outdoor spigot to let the line drain. Disconnect hoses,
because a connected hose holds water in the faucet regardless.

Do it when overnight lows start approaching freezing, not after the first hard freeze.

### 3. Seal exterior gaps

Warm air escapes and cold air enters through gaps you can find in twenty minutes: around window
frames, where pipes and cables enter the house, at the sill plate, and around exterior door
frames.

On a windy day, run the back of your hand slowly around each window and door frame. You will feel
the leaks. Caulk the gaps under a quarter inch, use expanding foam for larger ones, and add weather
stripping to doors.

This is the job with the fastest payback, because you feel the difference the same week.

### 4. Service the heating system

Replace the filter. Then run the heat for fifteen minutes before you actually need it, while it is
still warm enough to survive it not working.

Discovering a dead furnace on the first cold night means paying emergency rates and waiting. Finding
out in October means a normal appointment at a normal price.

### 5. Look at the roof from the ground

You do not need to get on it. Stand back with binoculars, or use your phone camera zoomed in.

You are looking for missing or lifted shingles, damaged flashing around chimneys and vents, and
sagging. Any of those found in fall is a repair. Found in February, it is an emergency.

## The rest, in rough priority order

| Task | Why it matters | Rough time |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse ceiling fans to clockwise | Pushes warm air down, noticeably cheaper heating | 5 min |
| Test smoke and CO alarms | Heating season is when CO incidents happen | 10 min |
| Clean dryer vent ducting | Lint plus heat is a genuine fire risk | 30 min |
| Trim branches near the roof | Ice-loaded branches break and land on shingles | 1 hr |
| Drain and store hoses | Split hoses are cheap but annoying | 15 min |
| Check attic insulation depth | Heat loss upward is the largest single loss | 20 min |
| Clean and cover outdoor furniture | Extends its life by years | 1 hr |

## The tool kit that covers most of it

You do not need a workshop. Eight tools cover the large majority of household jobs, fall
maintenance included.

- **Claw hammer**, 16oz, which is the size most people can control
- **Adjustable wrench**, for the outdoor faucet valve and everything plumbing-adjacent
- **Screwdriver set with interchangeable bits**, rather than a drawer of loose drivers
- **Pliers**, one regular and one needle-nose
- **Tape measure**, 25 foot
- **Utility knife** with replaceable blades
- **Cordless drill**, the one genuine upgrade over hand tools
- **Caulk gun**, needed for the sealing job above

A note on buying: avoid the 200-piece sets. The count is padded with sockets and bits you will
never use, and the core tools in them are usually the cheapest available. A 40-piece set with
decent tools beats a 200-piece set every time.

## Stud finders

Worth its own mention because it is the tool people skip and then regret.

Hanging anything heavy, mounting a TV, fitting a handrail, installing shelving: all of it needs a
stud. Guessing means holes in your drywall, and in older houses the 16 inch rule frequently does
not hold.

**What to look for:**

- **Deep scan mode**, for plaster walls and double drywall
- **Live wire detection**, which is a safety feature rather than a nicety
- **Surface calibration**, so it adjusts to your wall rather than assuming

## Do it in one afternoon

The five priority jobs take one afternoon between them if you have the tools ready. That is the
actual barrier for most people: starting a job, discovering you do not own a caulk gun, and
abandoning it.

Lay the tools out first. Then work outside to inside: gutters, faucets, roof check, then sealing,
then the furnace filter.

## The bottom line

Water and air. Gutters, outdoor faucets, sealing, heating, roof. Everything else on a fall
checklist is genuinely optional in a way those five are not.
